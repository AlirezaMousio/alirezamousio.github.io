Research Evolution

Over the past two years, this line of research has evolved through a series of connected works:

Anaraki, S. A. M., Croce, D., & Basili, R. (2025). Large language models for sustainability reporting: A systematic review and research agenda. Sustainable Futures, 10, 101494.

This work highlights key challenges in sustainability reporting, including lack of structured annotations, inconsistency across frameworks, and scalability issues. It shows how Artificial Intelligence, especially Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Large Language Models (LLMs), can play a crucial role in addressing these limitations.

    • From Research Methodology to Real-World ESG Analysis: UniTor ESG Insights

    Moving beyond methodological development and benchmark evaluation, my recent work also explores the practical deployment of ESG analysis technologies in real-world sustainability reporting scenarios. This direction led to the development of UniTor ESG Insights: An Interactive Human-in-the-Loop System for Traceable Sustainability Report Analysis, accepted as a demonstration paper at CIKM 2026, Rome, Italy.

    The demonstration system is presented through an interactive video: UniTor ESG Insights Demo Video .

    UniTor ESG Insights is a human-in-the-loop ESG analysis system that performs traceable, paragraph-level sustainability report analysis. Unlike traditional document-level ESG classifiers, the system is designed around a bottom-up evidence workflow, where every prediction and analytical decision can be traced back to the original paragraph-level evidence extracted from the source PDF reports.

    The system integrates ESG classification, information extraction, evidence retrieval, and explainable analysis capabilities to support transparent sustainability assessment. By combining automated NLP models with human validation, UniTor ESG Insights enables analysts to inspect, verify, and interpret ESG-related claims while maintaining a clear connection between model outputs and original reporting evidence.

    Beyond ESG Classification: Investigating Credibility Bias in ESG Language Models

    Expanding beyond ESG classification, information extraction, and greenwashing detection, I recently participated as a Visiting Researcher at the School of Computer Science, The University of Sheffield , Sheffield, England, United Kingdom (April 2026 – August 2026), under the supervision of Dr. Nafise Sadat Moosavi.

    During this research visit, I investigated credibility bias in ESG language models, focusing on how geographic, institutional, and linguistic factors influence automated sustainability assessment. The research explored methods for auditing contextual influence in LLM-based ESG evaluation by examining whether model judgments are primarily driven by sustainability evidence or by associated contextual signals.

    This work involved NLP and LLM-based approaches for ESG reporting, including model interpretability, credibility assessment, and the systematic analysis of contextual effects on sustainability-related language understanding. The goal is to improve the reliability, fairness, and transparency of AI-driven ESG assessment systems.
